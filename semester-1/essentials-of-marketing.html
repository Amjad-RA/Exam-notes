Essentials of Marketing

Marketing concepts, the 4Ps — product, price, place, promotion — and sustainable and green marketing. The product life cycle, channel levels and pricing methods are drawn the way you should reproduce them, and both papers are solved in examiner format. Main references: Kotler & Armstrong, Principles of Marketing; Sherlekar, Marketing Management; Ramaswamy & Namakumari.

💡 What the two papers tell you
  • Pricing is the single most-asked topic: pricing methods were a 10-mark essay (Univ) and a 6-mark question (Amal), plus competition-based and value-based pricing as 3-markers.
  • Green and sustainable marketing appeared in every section of both papers. Module IV has the most syllabus hours (15), and both 10-mark sets included a Module IV essay.
  • Marketing concepts (production → product → selling → marketing → societal) were asked in both papers, and societal marketing came up twice as a 3-marker.
  • The university paper spreads marks evenly across modules. Your college paper leaned hard on Module II: brand equity, product classification, PLC, warranties, pricing and new product development (NPD) together made up half the paper.

The 10-minute exam cheat sheet

Every definition, list and framework that earns marks — scan this outside the hall.

MODULE IIntroduction to marketing

  • Marketing (Kotler): “the process by which companies create value for customers and build strong customer relationships in order to capture value from customers in return.”
  • AMA (2017): “the activity, set of institutions, and processes for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners, and society at large.”
  • Five concepts (in historical order): Production (make it cheap and available) → Product (make it better) → Selling (push it hard) → Marketing (find needs, satisfy them better than rivals) → Societal (satisfy needs and protect society's long-run welfare).
  • Selling vs marketing (Levitt): selling focuses on the seller's need to convert product into cash; marketing focuses on the buyer's need to be satisfied. Selling: factory → product → push → profit through volume. Marketing: market → needs → integrated marketing → profit through satisfaction.
  • Marketing environment: Micro = company, suppliers, intermediaries, customers, competitors, publics. Macro = PESTLE (political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental) + demographic.
  • Characteristics: customer-oriented · exchange process · human activity · continuous · goal-oriented (satisfaction + profit) · integrated · both art and science · dynamic.

MODULE IIProduct & pricing

  • Marketing mix (McCarthy 4Ps, 1960): Product, Price, Place, Promotion. Services add People, Process, Physical evidence → 7Ps (Booms & Bitner).
  • Consumer products: convenience (soap, bread) · shopping (clothes, TV — compared on price, quality, style) · specialty (Rolex — special effort) · unsought (insurance). Industrial: materials & parts · capital items · supplies & services. Services: intangible, inseparable, variable, perishable.
  • NPD (8 steps): idea generation → screening → concept development & testing → marketing strategy → business analysis → product development → test marketing → commercialisation.
  • PLC: Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline (profits peak in growth / early maturity).
  • Brand equity = the value a brand adds to a product. Aaker's elements: brand awareness, perceived quality, brand associations, brand loyalty, other proprietary assets. Positioning = the place a brand occupies in the consumer's mind relative to rivals.
  • Warranty = seller's written promise to repair/replace if the product fails within a period (conditions apply). Guarantee = broader assurance, often money-back, usually free of charge.
  • Pricing objectives: survival · profit maximisation / target return · market-share leadership · quality leadership · meeting competition · stabilising prices. Factors: demand, competition, costs, objectives, government regulation, product stage.
  • Pricing methods: Cost-based (cost-plus, markup, break-even/target return) · Value-based (price set on perceived value to the buyer) · Competition-based (going-rate, sealed-bid). Tactics: skimming, penetration, psychological (₹99), discount, bundle.

MODULE IIIDistribution & promotion

  • Channel of distribution: a set of interdependent organisations (intermediaries) that make a product available for use or consumption by the consumer.
  • Channel levels: zero-level (direct: M → C) · one-level (M → R → C) · two-level (M → W → R → C) · three-level (M → Agent → W → R → C).
  • Functions: information · promotion · contact · matching (sorting, grading, packing) · negotiation · physical distribution (transport, storage) · financing · risk-taking.
  • Promotion mix: advertising · sales promotion · personal selling · public relations · direct/digital marketing.
  • Sales promotion: short-term incentives to encourage purchase now. Consumer: samples, coupons, cash refunds, premiums, contests, price packs (buy 1 get 1), loyalty points, POP displays. Trade: allowances, dealer contests, free goods.
  • Personal selling: face-to-face, two-way presentation by the sales force to make sales and build relationships. Features: personal confrontation, cultivation, response, flexibility.
  • Public relations: building good relations with the company's publics — favourable publicity, good corporate image, handling unfavourable rumours. Tools: press releases, events, sponsorships, CSR, websites.

MODULE IVSustainable marketing

  • Sustainable marketing (Kotler): socially and environmentally responsible marketing that meets the present needs of consumers and businesses while preserving or enhancing the ability of future generations to meet their needs.
  • Pillars: the triple bottom line — People (social), Planet (environmental), Profit (economic).
  • Kotler's five principles: consumer-oriented · customer-value · innovative · sense-of-mission · societal marketing.
  • Waste hierarchy: Reduce → Reuse → Recycle → Recover → Dispose. Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR), circular economy.
  • Resource depletion → higher input costs, supply risk, regulation. Mitigation: efficiency, renewables, recycled inputs, eco-design, circular models, sustainable sourcing.
  • Energy consumption: energy used in production, transport, storage and product use. Cut it with efficient equipment, renewables, logistics optimisation, BEE star labels.
  • Green marketing: marketing products and practices on the basis of environmental benefits — eco-friendly product, green packaging, honest green claims, eco-labels. Avoid greenwashing. Examples: Tata Motors EVs, ITC paperboards, Body Shop.
