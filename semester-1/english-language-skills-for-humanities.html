💡 What the three papers tell you
  • Your college paper follows the syllabus weights; the university papers do not. Amal College gave Modules III and IV 16 marks each — the university gave Module IV nothing. Prepare all four modules evenly.
  • “Anthem” and “Rip it Out” were asked in all three papers. Treat them as certain.
  • Section A is never from the syllabus texts. It is an unseen passage — caffeine science (2024), Tagore on nationalism (2025), girls' education and Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (Amal 2025). Comprehension technique is the only preparation — see Section 3.
  • Ambedkar's speech is the only text never asked. It carries Module IV weight, so expect it next.
  • Questions repeat by text, not by wording: Anthem was asked as “religious themes”, “central messages” and “central themes”; Rip it Out as “individual vs society”, “effective speaker” and “humour”. Know each text from several angles.

The 10-minute exam cheat sheet

Author · year · form · one-line gist · the quotes to drop · the themes. Twelve texts, scannable outside the hall.

MODULE IListening

  • I Have a Dream — Martin Luther King Jr. · speech · 28 Aug 1963, Lincoln Memorial, March on Washington. America's “promissory note” returned as a “bad check” marked “insufficient funds”. Non-violent “soul force”; children “judged … by the content of their character”. Devices: anaphora, metaphor, Biblical allusion. → Civil Rights Act 1964, Voting Rights Act 1965.
  • Anthem — Leonard Cohen (Canadian, 1934–2016) · song · album The Future, 1992. Refrain: “There is a crack in everything / That's how the light gets in.” Themes: imperfection as grace, hope, living in the present, hypocrisy of power, peace “bought and sold”.
  • Art as Social Work — Amy Morales Lara · TEDxCUNY talk (2023). Bronx-born daughter of immigrants, training in social work at CUNY Hunter College. Art + social work → curiosity about people and places, being a supportive helper, voice and healing for marginalised communities.

MODULE IISpeaking

  • On Soft Language — George Carlin (US, 1937–2008) · stand-up, HBO special Doin' It Again (1990). Euphemisms are “words that hide the truth”. Shell shock → battle fatigue → operational exhaustion → post-traumatic stress disorder: “the pain is completely buried under jargon”. Also toilet paper → bathroom tissue, sneakers → running shoes, dump → landfill, slums → substandard housing, old → senior citizens, die → pass away. Stand-up = social criticism: laughter lowers defences.
  • Death of a Salesman (Act I, opening scene) — Arthur Miller · play, 1949, Pulitzer Prize. Willy Loman, 60-plus salesman, returns home “tired to the death”; Linda soothes him; Biff (34) “lost”; Swiss cheese; boxed-in by apartments; the 1928 Chevvy. Themes: American Dream, illusion vs reality, family, ageing.
  • “Rip it Out” — Dead Poets Society (1989), dir. Peter Weir; Robin Williams as John Keating, Welton Academy, 1959. Pritchard's graph of poetry → “Excrement… Rip it out!”; “a battle, a war”; “think for yourselves”; Whitman → “What will your verse be?”

MODULE IIIReading

  • The Second Coming — W. B. Yeats (Irish, Nobel 1923) · poem, written 1919, published 1920. “Turning and turning in the widening gyre / The falcon cannot hear the falconer; / Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold.” “The best lack all conviction, while the worst / Are full of passionate intensity.” A sphinx-like “rough beast … slouches towards Bethlehem”. Post-WWI chaos; cyclical history (gyres).
  • The Frog Prince — James Finn Garner, Politically Correct Bedtime Stories (1994) · satirical retelling. The frog is a swindling property developer punished by a spell; restored, he wants to develop more land; the princess rejects the “male power structures in the castle” (marriage, property, patriarchy) and ends his schemes by killing him → the eco-feminist reversal of the fairy tale.
  • Optimism, Part I “Optimism Within” — Helen Keller (1880–1968) · essay, 1903. Deaf-blind from 19 months; freed by “a little word from the fingers of another”. “Darkness cannot shut me in again. I have had a glimpse of the shore.” “The struggle which evil necessitates is one of the greatest blessings.” Optimism without knowing evil is “a house builded on sand”. Rejects the “dangerous optimism of ignorance and indifference”; believes in “the preponderance of good”.

MODULE IVWriting

  • Annihilation of Caste, Section 4 — Dr B. R. Ambedkar · speech prepared for the 1936 Jat-Pat-Todak Mandal conference, Lahore (cancelled; self-published). Caste is “not merely division of labour… also a division of labourers”: graded hierarchy, not aptitude, blocks job mobility → unemployment, no choice → “neither men's hearts nor their minds are in their work”.
  • The World-Renowned Nose — Vaikom Muhammad Basheer (“Beypore Sultan”, 1908–1994) · Malayalam satire Vishwavikhyathamaya Mookku, 1954. Mookken the illiterate cook's nose grows “level with his navel” → fame, money, films → parties fight over him → “rubber nose” scandal → experts from 48 countries prick it → “Mookashri”, MP. Satire on mass psychology, media, politics.
  • For the Love of Cinema — review of Cinema Paradiso (1988), dir. Giuseppe Tornatore. Film director Salvatore (Totò) returns to his Sicilian village for projectionist Alfredo's funeral; censored kisses, fire, lost love Elena; Alfredo's reel of kisses. Ennio Morricone score; Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film. A model of review writing.

Literary & rhetorical terms to use by name

AnaphoraRepeating words at the start of lines — “I have a dream…”, “Let freedom ring”
Metaphor“bad check”, “crack in everything”, “widening gyre”
SymbolFalcon/falconer, holy dove, Willy's car, the ripped page
AllusionBible, Lincoln (“Five score years ago”), Whitman
EuphemismA mild word that hides a harsh truth (Carlin)
SatireRidicule to expose folly — Basheer, Garner, Carlin
ParodyImitation of a form to mock it — Garner's fairy tales
Irony · paradoxBrokenness lets light in; a nose wins a national award
Hyperbole · caricatureMookken's nose; “experts from forty-eight countries”
Rhetorical question“What will your verse be?”
Ethos · pathos · logosCredibility · emotion · reason in persuasion
RefrainA repeated chorus — Cohen's “There is a crack…”

If you revise only these

  1. Unseen-passage method: answer in your own words, in full sentences, from the passage only
  2. Anthem and Rip it Out — asked in all three papers (themes, humour, effective speaking)
  3. MLK: context of 1963 + “bad check” + three famous dream lines + relevance today
  4. “Anthem”: the refrain, the dove, the bells, “killers in high places”
  5. Amy Morales Lara: art as voice, healing and community; curiosity; being a helper
  6. “Rip it Out”: Pritchard vs Keating, conformity vs individuality, “What will your verse be?”
  7. Willy Loman's character + family relationships (Linda, Biff, Happy)
  8. Ambedkar Section 4: the five arguments against caste as division of labour
  9. Basheer: the rise-fall-rise of Mookken — social criticism and humour (Amal 10-mark question)
  10. Yeats: falcon/falconer, gyre, rough beast; Keller: true vs false optimism
  11. Write a short review of Cinema Paradiso (Amal 2025) — title, details, plot, craft, verdict
Memory hooks
  • Listening = Dream · Crack · Canvas (MLK, Cohen, Morales Lara)
  • Speaking = Euphemism · Salesman · Rip (Carlin, Miller, Weir)
  • Reading = Falcon · Frog · Fingers (Yeats, Garner, Keller)
  • Writing = Caste · Nose · Cinema (Ambedkar, Basheer, Tornatore)
