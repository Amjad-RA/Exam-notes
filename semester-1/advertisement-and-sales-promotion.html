10-minute cheat sheet

Scan this outside the exam hall. Every line is a definition, list or name that earns marks.

MODULE IIntroduction to advertising

  • Advertising (AMA): “any paid form of non-personal presentation and promotion of ideas, goods or services by an identified sponsor.”
  • Nature: paid · non-personal · mass communication · identified sponsor · persuasive · one-way · art + science · part of the promotion mix.
  • Scope: products, services, ideas, institutions, persons, social causes.
  • Objectives (Kotler): Inform → Persuade → Remind (+ reinforce). Also: launch products, build image, support dealers, meet competition.
  • Importance to 4 groups: manufacturers · consumers · middlemen · society.
  • Types (5 bases): purpose (product, institutional, comparative, co-operative, public service) · audience (consumer, B2B, trade, professional) · area (local → global) · media (print, broadcast, outdoor, digital) · demand (primary vs selective).
  • DAGMAR = Defining Advertising Goals for Measured Advertising Results — Russell H. Colley, 1961. Stages ACCA: Awareness → Comprehension → Conviction → Action.
  • Budget methods: % of sales · affordable · competitive parity · objective & task (best) · arbitrary · unit of sales · ROI.
  • Limitations: raises price · misleading · creates artificial wants · favours big firms · offensive content · effect hard to measure.

MODULE IIMessage & media

  • Copy = all written/spoken matter in an ad. Body copy = main text after the headline that explains and persuades.
  • Components: Headline · Sub-headline · Body copy · Illustration · Slogan · Logo/signature · Call to action (+ layout).
  • Appeals: Rational (price, quality, economy) · Emotional (fear, humour, love, pride, nostalgia) · Moral (social causes).
  • Effective copy: AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action · one clear USP · KISS · benefits > features · strong CTA.
  • Media: print · TV · radio · outdoor (OOH) · cinema · direct mail · digital (search, display, social, video, email, mobile).
  • Media selection factors: target audience · product · reach & frequency · cost (CPM) · budget · objective · message · competitors · timing · law.
  • Digital strengths: precise targeting · measurable (CTR) · low cost / pay-per-click · interactive · real-time changes · retargeting.
  • Trends: social media ads · influencer marketing · Reels/Shorts · programmatic & AI ads · AR try-ons · native ads · OTT · voice search.
  • Influencer tiers: nano (1K–10K) · micro (10K–100K) · macro (100K–1M) · mega (1M+). ASCI 2021 rule: disclose with #ad.

MODULE IIIEffectiveness & ethics

  • Dual objectives: communication effect (awareness, attitude — easier to measure) vs sales effect (sales, market share — harder: time-lag, other factors).
  • Sales effect methods: historical (past sales vs ad spend) · experimental (test markets).
  • Pre-tests: consumer jury · portfolio · checklist · readability · physiological (eye camera, psychogalvanometer, tachistoscope) · focus groups.
  • Post-tests: recall (aided / unaided / day-after) · recognition (Starch: noted, associated, read most) · inquiry · sales · attitude · digital metrics.
  • Ethics: truth · substantiated claims · decency · no surrogate ads · protect children · fair comparison · disclose endorsements.
  • ASCI = Advertising Standards Council of India · 1985 · Mumbai · voluntary self-regulation. Code: honest · decent · not harmful · fair. Complaints → Consumer Complaints Council (CCC).
  • Law: Consumer Protection Act 2019 → CCPA can stop misleading ads; penalty up to ₹10 lakh (₹50 lakh repeat); endorsers barred 1–3 years.

MODULE IVSales promotion

  • Sales promotion (Kotler): “short-term incentives to encourage the purchase or sale of a product or service.”
  • Nature: short-term · incentive · immediate response · supplements ads & selling · aimed at consumers, trade, sales force.
  • Role in the mix: part of Promotion (4th P); turns ad-created awareness into action; bridges advertising and personal selling.
  • Objectives: induce trial · lift short-term sales · win brand-switchers · reward loyalty · clear stock · counter rivals · off-season sales · dealer support · motivate sales force.
  • Consumer tools: samples · coupons · price-offs · premiums · refunds/cashback · contests · sweepstakes · games · bonus packs · exchange offers · loyalty points · POP displays.
  • Trade tools: conventions · conferences · trade fairs · exhibitions · fashion shows · specialties · novelties · allowances · dealer contests · push money.
  • Strategy cycle: objective → target → tool → incentive size → conditions → duration → distribution → budget → pre-test → run → evaluate.
  • Ads vs SP: long-term image vs short-term sales · reason to buy vs incentive to buy now.

1.4Types of advertising — classify by five bases

Product advertising

Promotes a specific product's features and benefits to raise its sales. Pioneering → competitive → reminder over the life cycle.

e.g., a smartphone launch ad listing camera and battery features.

Institutional advertising

Promotes the company — its image, values, CSR — not one product. Builds long-term trust.

e.g., Tata Steel's “We also make steel” campaign on its community work.

Comparative advertising

Compares the brand with a named or implied rival to claim superiority. Must be truthful and not denigrate (ASCI Chapter IV).

e.g., Rin vs Tide (2010); the Pepsi Challenge.

1.5DAGMAR — setting measurable advertising objectives

DAGMAR (Defining Advertising Goals for Measured Advertising Results) was developed by Russell H. Colley in 1961 for the Association of National Advertisers (USA). Core idea: advertising's job is communication, not sales, so its goals must be written as measurable communication tasks.

★ Colley's definition of an advertising goal
“A specific communication task, to be accomplished among a defined audience, to a given degree, in a given period of time.”
A DAGMAR objective must be…
  1. Concrete & measurable — a clear communication task
  2. For a defined target audience
  3. Benchmarked — starting point and degree of change stated
  4. Time-bound — a fixed period
  5. Written down — agreed by client and agency

Example: “Raise awareness of Brand X among college students in Kerala from 20% to 60% within 6 months.”

Colley's 6 Ms

Merchandise · Markets · Motives · Messages · Media · Measurements — the checklist used to plan a DAGMAR campaign.

Why it matters

It is the basis of the objective-and-task budget and lets results be measured, so it links Module I to Module III.

Merits
  • Clear, written, measurable goals
  • Results can be evaluated
  • Better client–agency coordination
  • Logical base for budgeting
Criticisms
  • Ignores the sales objective
  • Buyers don't always move step-by-step (impulse buys)
  • Measurement is costly
  • Can restrict creativity

Logic and self-interest.

Sense of right and wrong.

Module III · 8 hours · 15 marks

Advertising Effectiveness & Ethics

Communication & sales effectsPre-testingPost-testingEthics in IndiaASCI
★ Golden definition
“Advertising effectiveness is the extent to which an advertisement achieves its predetermined communication objectives (awareness, comprehension, attitude) and sales objectives (sales volume, market share), measured through pre-testing and post-testing.”

3.1The dual objectives: communication effects vs sales effects

Communication effects
  • Impact on awareness, knowledge, attitude, preference
  • Measured by recall, recognition, attitude tests
  • Easier and quicker to measure
  • Measured before and after release
  • Linked to DAGMAR goals
Sales effects
  • Impact on sales volume, revenue, market share
  • Measured by historical and experimental methods
  • Hard to isolate: price, distribution, competition also act
  • Carry-over / lag effect — sales come months later
  • Easier for direct-response and online ads
Historical method

Correlates past advertising spend with past sales using statistics — shows how sales moved as spending changed.

Experimental method

Test markets get different ad spends (high / normal / zero); the difference in their sales shows the ad's effect.

3.2Methods of measuring advertising effectiveness

Why pre-test?It catches a weak ad before money is spent on media. Post-testing tells you what the money achieved and guides the next campaign.

3.3Ethical aspects of advertising in India

Advertising ethics are the moral principles of truth, decency and fairness that govern what an advertiser says and shows. Unethical ads may be legal yet still harm consumers or society.

Misleading / false claimsUnproven “cures” or “100% germ kill” claims. In 2024 the Supreme Court acted against Patanjali's misleading medicinal ads (IMA case).

Consumer Protection Act, 2019 — the numbers to quote
  • Sec 2(28) defines misleading advertisement; Sec 10 sets up the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA).
  • Sec 21: CCPA can order an ad withdrawn or modified; penalty on manufacturer or endorser up to ₹10 lakh (repeat: ₹50 lakh); endorser can be barred for up to 1 year (repeat: 3 years).
  • Sec 89: false or misleading ads by a manufacturer or service provider — up to 2 years jail and ₹10 lakh fine (repeat: 5 years and ₹50 lakh).

3.4ASCI — Advertising Standards Council of India

Fact file
  • Set up in 1985, head office Mumbai
  • Voluntary self-regulatory body of advertisers, agencies, media
  • Non-profit company (Sec 25, Companies Act 1956 — now Sec 8, 2013 Act)
  • Administers the ASCI Code for Self-Regulation of Advertising Content
  • Complaints decided by the Consumer Complaints Council (CCC)
  • Under the Cable TV Network Rules, TV ads must follow the ASCI Code
The four principles of the ASCI Code
  1. Honesty & truthfulness — claims must be true and substantiated
  2. Decency — not offensive to public standards
  3. Not harmful — no promotion of hazardous products or unsafe acts
  4. Fairness in competition — no denigration of rivals
Functions
  • Frames and updates the Code
  • Handles consumer complaints
  • Monitors ads suo motu
  • Issues sector guidelines

Explain the methods for measuring advertising effectiveness. / How can we measure advertising effectiveness?›6 marks★ Asked 3×

Asked in: Model 2024 Q15 · Nov 2024 Q18 · Nov 2025 Q15

Advertising effectiveness is the extent to which an ad achieves its communication and sales objectives. It is measured before release (pre-testing), during the campaign (concurrent testing) and after release (post-testing).

A. Pre-testing (before release)
  1. Consumer jury test — a panel of target consumers ranks alternative ads; the best is chosen.
  2. Portfolio test — the test ad is placed among others in a folder; respondents' recall of it is checked.
  3. Checklist method — the ad is rated against a list of desirable qualities.
  4. Readability test — the Flesch formula checks how easy the copy is to read.
  5. Physiological tests — eye camera (eye movement), psychogalvanometer (emotional arousal), tachistoscope (speed of recognition), pupillometer.
B. Concurrent testing (during the campaign)

Coincidental surveys, consumer diaries and tracking studies; live digital dashboards.

C. Post-testing (after release)
  1. Recall tests — unaided and aided recall; Day-After Recall (Burke).
  2. Recognition test — Starch readership test classifies readers as noted, associated and read most.
  3. Inquiry test — counts enquiries or coupons returned.
  4. Sales test — compares sales before and after, or in test vs control markets.
  5. Attitude test — measures change in brand image and preference.
  6. Digital metrics — impressions, click-through rate, conversions, return on ad spend.

Pre-testing prevents costly mistakes; post-testing shows what the money achieved and guides the next campaign.

Explain the DAGMAR approach to setting advertising objectives.›6 marks★ Asked 3×

Asked in: Nov 2024 Q13 · Nov 2025 Q18 · Model 2024 Q2 (3m)

DAGMAR — Defining Advertising Goals for Measured Advertising Results — was developed by Russell H. Colley in 1961 for the Association of National Advertisers (USA). It treats advertising as a communication task and insists on measurable goals.

Advertising goal (Colley): “a specific communication task, to be accomplished among a defined audience, to a given degree, in a given period of time.”

Communication spectrum (ACCA)
  1. Unawareness — the consumer has not heard of the brand.
  2. Awareness — knows the brand exists.
  3. Comprehension — understands what it is and what it does.
  4. Conviction — is mentally convinced to buy.
  5. Action — actually buys.
Characteristics of a DAGMAR objective
  • Concrete and measurable communication task
  • Defined target audience
  • Benchmark and degree of change (e.g., awareness from 20% to 60%)
  • Specified time period
  • Written down and agreed

Merits: clear goals, measurable results, basis for the objective-and-task budget. Limitations: ignores sales, buyers may skip steps (impulse buying), measurement is costly.

DAGMAR made advertising accountable by turning vague hopes into measurable communication goals.

Explain the various methods for determining the advertising budget allocation.›6 marks★ Asked 2×

Asked in: Model 2024 Q13 · Nov 2024 Q15

The advertising budget is the amount a firm sets aside for advertising over a period. Common methods:

  1. Percentage of sales method — a fixed % of past or expected sales (e.g., 5% of ₹8 crore = ₹40 lakh). Simple, but wrongly treats sales as the cause of advertising.
  2. Affordable method — whatever remains after all other expenses. Suits small firms but ignores what advertising can achieve.
  3. Competitive parity method — match competitors' spending or share of voice. Avoids ad wars but assumes rivals are right.
  4. Objective and task method — set objectives, list tasks, cost them; the total is the budget. Most logical and linked to DAGMAR, but costs are hard to estimate.
  5. Arbitrary method — decided by management's judgement. Quick but unscientific.
  6. Unit of sales method — a fixed amount per unit sold or expected; common for cars and durables.
  7. Return on investment method — advertising is treated as an investment; spend where the return is highest.

The objective-and-task method is considered best; in practice firms combine it with percentage of sales and competitive parity.
